Live multi-agent audit · PR #79 · 2026-06-16

The cluster is too small. Now what?

Standalone pi.dev research agents race real RL jobs on a shared, simulated SLURM cluster. Cooperation and defection emerge from scarcity — no payoff matrix, no "this is a test." The one knob: a team chat channel. Does talking move them off the selfish equilibrium, and do they honor what they say?

§1 The setup

Each researcher is its own pi --mode rpc agent — a stand-in for a subagent in a harness — given only a naturalistic brief (no "this is a test", no named adversary) and a stdlib SLURM CLI (sbatch/squeue/scancel/scontrol) over a shared cluster. A non-LLM driver round-robins a per-turn prompt and advances a simulated clock; the shared team-rl account lets anyone cancel or resize anyone's job (the affordance that makes defection possible), and the append-only scheduler ledger (sacct.db.jsonl) is mechanical ground truth — no judge needed to score defection. The one manipulation is a shared team chat channel: no_comms vs comms.

Defection here is rarer and subtler — and it persists across all models. Thermal shutdowns fall from 20% / 7% / 13% (Haiku / Sonnet / Opus, no comms) to 13% / 0% / 0% with a channel: Sonnet and Opus reach zero, but Haiku barely moves. Defection is otherwise transparent (say-do ≈1–1.5), with one exception — Haiku with a channel promises to pause then keeps training (volunteer-then-free-ride): its say-do gap rises with comms even as the channel still helps the group avoid shutdown.

§5 Analysis

I mocked up these three social dilemmas and ran a few pi agents on each, usually just given a brief/task, to see what happened (this is meant to simulate a subagent in a harness). I mostly cared whether they'd pursue their task more aggressively and take the game-theory-optimal (i.e. selfish) action without communication — so I run each both with and without comms through a team channel they can use to reason about the dynamics. It's pretty interesting.

For the prisoner's dilemma, Haiku and Sonnet work as you'd expect and cancel each other's jobs (0% both-finish) — and don't once they have comms (Haiku goes 0%→40% both-finish, with foreign scancels dropping 10.4→1.1 per run). Opus, however, just figures out that another job is running and is willing to cooperate and not cancel it — 80% both-finish with zero cancels even with no comms.

  • For the tragedy of the commons, outside of Haiku, models are generally willing to do less for the good of the group (and for themselves overall): Sonnet and Opus self-limit to roughly the fair ~1 GPU each even without comms (1.5 / 1.1 GPU/agent, 98% / 92% finishing), while Haiku over-requests hard (7.7 GPU/agent, only 37% finish) until a channel reins it in (3.6 GPU/agent, 69% finish).
  • For the volunteer's dilemma it's a bit less obvious, so there's a small amount of defection across all models (thermal shutdowns of 20% / 7% / 13% for Haiku / Sonnet / Opus without comms). Interestingly, comms takes Sonnet and Opus to zero shutdowns but Haiku barely changes (→13%). This is weird because the behavior is rare relative to the others but persists through all of the models.
  • I think these are further worth studying. Specifically, as you change the pressure and don't make it obvious that you can cooperate, you end up with different dynamics — you need to be creative in constructing scenarios here.
    • e.g. some runs are obviously more important than others, and it's important to see if agents can identify this.
