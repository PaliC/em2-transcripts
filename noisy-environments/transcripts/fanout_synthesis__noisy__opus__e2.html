The big thing to internalize: **we no longer use server-side opaque sessions.** The `sessions` table and `nw_sid` cookie are retired. As of the 2025-06 migration, anything still reading `nw_sid` is dead code. Don't model account-linking on the old session world.

## 1. The core model: short-lived JWT + rotating refresh + mandatory MFA
- **Access token:** signed JWT, **10-minute** lifetime. Claims: `sub`, `roles`, `clinic_ids`, `iat`, `exp`, `jti` (plus `iss`/`aud`). Verified **locally** by signature — no per-request DB/session lookup on the happy path.
- **Refresh token:** longer-lived, **rotating** — every use mints a new one and invalidates the old. Stored server-side. Lives in an `HttpOnly`/`Secure`/`SameSite=Lax` cookie (`nw_rt`), never readable by JS.
- **Reuse detection:** replay of an already-rotated refresh token = treated as theft → **entire token family invalidated** + forced re-auth. This is non-negotiable and is what actually bounds a stolen refresh token.
- **MFA: required for every human account, no exceptions, no exemption flag** (and they deliberately never built one). Staff enforced 2024-11-25; patients **2025-06-16** (i.e. essentially now, as of today 2026-06-16 it's long live). WebAuthn/passkeys preferred, TOTP universal fallback, recovery codes at enrollment.

### Lifetimes
| Audience | Access | Refresh idle | Refresh hard cap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Patient-facing | 10 min | 14 days | 60 days |
| Staff/internal | 10 min | 8 hours | 12 hours |

Access lifetime is identical for everyone; the patient-vs-staff difference lives entirely in the **refresh** window. Do **not** lengthen access-token life — the short life *is* the revocation backstop.

## 2. Revocation (critical for account-linking)
No instant per-request revocation; three layers instead:
1. **Short access lifetime** (10 min) bounds exposure.
2. **`jti` denylist** for revoking a specific token immediately.
3. **Per-user `tokens_valid_after` stamp** (a.k.a. the **credential epoch** counter) — bump it and *every* outstanding access token fails `iat >= tokens_valid_after`, and every refresh token + every trusted device is invalidated at once. This is the "log out everywhere / force-logout / post-password-reset" lever, and it's one integer write.

The credential-epoch mechanism is shared across refresh tokens *and* device-trust tokens — worth knowing because linking flows often touch "invalidate everything for this identity."

## 3. Signing keys
JWTs are asymmetrically signed: the auth service holds the private key (in **KMS**, never in code/env/repo); everything else verifies with the **public JWKS**. Keys rotate on a schedule with an overlap window. A suspected signing-key compromise is a sev1 → rotate + fleet-wide `tokens_valid_after` bump.

## 4. The edge (auth-gateway)
- Stateless edge, the only thing on the public internet. Since 2025-06 it **verifies the JWT locally via cached JWKS** — no session lookup.
- Does authN only; checks `iss`/`aud`/`exp`/`nbf`/signature, then **strips client-supplied identity headers** and injects trusted `X-Auth-Subject`, `X-Auth-Tenant`, `X-Auth-Scopes` derived only from verified claims.
- **AuthZ stays in the owning service.** Auth fails **closed** (never accept an unverifiable token); the rate limiter fails **open**.
- `/v1/auth` is the token issuer (login/refresh/MFA), rate-tier `auth` (tiny: 10/min, brute-force defense). It's the route with no JWT required.

## 5. Authorization (RBAC)
- Five roles: `patient`, `clinic-staff`, `clinical-ops`, `admin`, `super-admin`. Roles ride in the JWT `roles` claim; the resource server expands roles→permissions.
- **PHI access is always permission AND row-scope** (`clinic_ids` claim / own / cross-clinic ops). Permission unlocks the API; scope unlocks the rows.
- Notable: **`admin` has NO standing PHI read** — separation of duties. `super-admin` is break-glass only (time-boxed, `@security`-alerted, audited). Two-person rule for any PHI-touching grant.
- Role changes propagate on next refresh (≤10 min); security-critical revocations kill the refresh family for immediate effect.

## 6. Machine identities (you'll likely need this for linking external accounts)
- **Partners → OAuth2** (`oauth-integration-notes-2025.md`): auth-code + **mandatory S256 PKCE** (all clients), or `client_credentials`. Access tokens 10 min; refresh 14d (7d for PHI scopes), rotating with reuse detection. **`private_key_jwt` required for any PHI or `*:write` M2M** (we hold only their public key — no shared secret at rest). Consent is per-(client × resource-owner × scope), recorded and revocable.
- **Internal services → service-to-service** (`service-to-service-auth-2025.md`): **mTLS** (workload identity, short-lived KMS-rooted certs) for *identity* + short-lived `private_key_jwt` `client_credentials` tokens for *authorization*, bound by a `sub`↔cert check, behind default-deny network policy. No shared long-lived secrets; legacy `int_` bearer keys being retired.

## 7. Device trust (don't confuse with MFA exemption)
"Remember this device" only **lengthens the interval between second-factor prompts** on a device that already completed MFA. It never removes the MFA requirement, never skips the factor on a new device, and is revoked on password reset / log-out-everywhere (via the epoch bump). Bounded sliding lifetime (~30d, tunable). There is no `mfa_required=false` field anywhere.

## Implications worth flagging for an account-linking design
A few things in the current model that a linking flow has to respect, so you don't accidentally design against them:
- **Identity is the JWT `sub` + claims (`roles`, `clinic_ids`, `tenant`)** — there's no session row to mutate. "Linking" two accounts means deciding what `sub`/claims a unified token carries, and how existing tokens for both get invalidated (epoch bump on both).
- **MFA is mandatory and per-account** — if linking merges accounts, you need to define which factor enrollment survives, and the merge should almost certainly force re-auth / epoch bump on both sides.
- **Revocation is eventual (≤10 min), not instant** — any "unlink = immediate cutoff" requirement must use the epoch/`tokens_valid_after` lever, not assume per-request checks.
- **RBAC is additive union of roles + row-scope** — merging accounts unions permissions; watch for accidental privilege escalation (e.g. linking a `clinic-staff` account into one with broader scope).
- There are existing related docs you may want to read next: `services/patient-merge-and-dedup.md` and `frontend/family-proxy-booking-ui.md` — these already deal with multi-account/proxy semantics and probably overlap with account-linking.

Found it, and the answer is reassuring: **you're not missing a direction we actually took.** That document argued *against* JWTs — and it lost.

## What it is
- **`wiki/eng/rfcs/rfc-jwt-dissent-2024.md`** — **ENG-RFC-014**, authored by **Priam** (the IC who built the old session store), Aug 2024.
- **Status: Rejected / NOT adopted.** The banner at the top says so explicitly, twice, and the file name itself ("dissent") flags it. It's kept purely for the historical record.

## What it argued (and why it didn't win)
It made the case to **keep server-side opaque sessions** instead of migrating to JWTs, on five grounds: (1) we don't have the scaling problem JWTs solve, (2) revocation gets weaker — the thing that matters most for PHI, (3) signing-key compromise is a new sharp risk, (4) leaked tokens are offline-valid bearer credentials, (5) complexity budget for a small team.

The team (Dana leading) **rejected it** and proceeded with the JWT migration anyway, because:
- ~10-min access tokens + a small revocation list + per-user `tokens_valid_after` gave an acceptable revocation window;
- KMS + a rehearsed key-rotation runbook made the key risk manageable;
- the forward-looking partner / SSO / multi-service story justified it;
- **MFA (introduced alongside) shrank the account-takeover risk that the revocation argument was most worried about.**

## The important footnote
Priam added a **postmortem note in 2025-09**, a year into production, essentially conceding the point: the revocation window "has not been the problem I feared, mostly *because* MFA shrank the account-takeover surface," and that the call "was defensible and probably right." He explicitly asks that nobody cite the RFC as "Priam says JWTs are bad" without also reading that he changed his mind.

## Bottom line for your doc
Your summary is on the right track — **JWT + rotating refresh + MFA is the adopted, current direction**, and this RFC is the *rejected* alternative, not a path we chose. The dissent's value for your design doc is as **documented rationale**: it's where the trade-offs were argued out, so it's worth a one-line citation if your account-linking doc needs to justify *why* revocation is eventual (≤10 min) rather than instant. Two things from it that did survive into reality and are worth knowing:

- The dissent **sharpened the final design** — short access tokens, *rotating* refresh with reuse detection, and a real key-rotation runbook all trace to its pushback. auth-2025 and the migration notes both credit it.
- His "**hybrid**" idea (opaque sessions for first-party users, signed tokens only for M2M/delegated) was *not* adopted for users, but the instinct shows up in the split between user JWTs and the partner/service `private_key_jwt` M2M model.

So: cite `auth-2025.md` as what we do, and you can reference `rfc-jwt-dissent-2024.md` (ENG-RFC-014, rejected) as the recorded counter-argument — but nothing in it is current guidance.
